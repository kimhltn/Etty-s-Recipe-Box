# Ragù alla Bolognese — Cook Mode

*Plan-ahead note: the sauce wants 3+ hours at the laziest possible simmer — start by 5:30 to eat at 9:30, or cook it tonight and eat it tomorrow (it's genuinely better on day two). Sauce keeps 4–5 days in the fridge: cook a portion of fresh pasta per night and it's four dinners from one pot.*

**Serves:** 4 over pasta (solo: 4 nights of dinners) | **Active:** ~40 min | **Simmer:** 3–4 hr | **Total:** ~4 hr wall clock

---

## 🛒 Grocery List

### Produce
- [ ] 1 small yellow onion (need ½ cup chopped fine)
- [ ] 1 rib celery (need ⅔ cup chopped fine)
- [ ] 1–2 carrots (need ⅔ cup chopped fine)

### Meat & Dairy
- [ ] ¾ lb (~350 g) ground beef chuck — *coarse grind if the butcher offers; 80/20, not lean*
- [ ] Butter (3 tbsp + 1 tbsp for the pasta)
- [ ] Whole milk (1 cup)
- [ ] Parmigiano-Reggiano — a block, for the table **and its rind for the pot**

### Pantry
- [ ] Vegetable oil (1 tbsp)
- [ ] 1 cup dry white wine
- [ ] 1 (400 g / 14-oz) can whole Italian plum tomatoes (need 1½ cups with juice)
- [ ] Tomato paste (2 tbsp)
- [ ] Fresh tagliatelle or pappardelle (~125 g per serving, bought per night or for the fridge)
- [ ] Kosher salt, black pepper, whole nutmeg

---

## Before You Start (10-Minute Prep)

- **Soffritto:** chop the onion, celery, and carrot *fine* — the only real knife work of the night. *(Shortcut: pulse in a food processor to a fine chop, not a purée.)*
- **Tomatoes:** roughly chop the canned tomatoes in their juice (kitchen shears straight into the can works).
- **Parmesan rind:** cut the rind off the block; give it a quick rinse.
- **Equipment:** one heavy Dutch oven or deep saucepan does the whole sauce.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Soffritto (10 min)

1. Warm **1 tbsp vegetable oil** and **3 tbsp butter** in the pot over medium. Cook the **½ cup chopped onion** until translucent, about **5 minutes**.
2. Add the **⅔ cup celery** and **⅔ cup carrot** and cook **2–3 minutes**, until coated and glossy.

### Step 2 — Meat, Then Milk (~40 min, mostly passive)

1. Add the **¾ lb ground chuck**, a **big pinch of kosher salt**, and **a few grinds of pepper**. Crumble with a spoon and cook only until the meat has lost its raw red color — **no browning**; this is the un-seared braise family, and browning here makes the finished sauce grainy instead of silky.
2. Add **1 cup whole milk** and simmer gently, stirring now and then, until it has bubbled fully away — **20–30 minutes**. Grate in **about ⅛ tsp nutmeg** and stir. *(Milk before wine is Hazan's signature — it protects the meat's tenderness against the wine's acidity.)*

### Step 3 — Wine (~20–30 min, passive)

1. Add **1 cup dry white wine** and simmer until fully evaporated, **20–30 minutes**. The pot should look like meat glistening in fat, not meat in liquid, before you move on — each evaporation genuinely has to finish; that's where the depth comes from.

### Step 4 — Fry the Paste, Then the Long Lazy Simmer (3+ hr)

1. Push the meat aside, add **2 tbsp tomato paste** to a clear patch, and fry it, stirring, **1–2 minutes**, until it darkens a shade and smells sweet rather than tinny. Stir it through the meat.
2. Add the **1½ cups chopped tomatoes with their juice** and tuck in the **parmesan rind**. When it starts to bubble, drop the heat to the absolute minimum — the target is *an occasional lazy bubble breaking the surface*, uncovered.
3. Simmer **3 hours or more**, stirring occasionally. If it dries out before time is up, add **½ cup water** as needed; by the end there should be **no watery liquid left, and the fat should separate and gloss the surface** — meat coated in sauce, not meat in liquid. Fish out what's left of the rind.
4. Taste and adjust **salt** — it usually wants another pinch.

### Step 5 — Pasta (per meal, 5 min)

1. Boil **~125 g fresh tagliatelle** per serving in well-salted water until just shy of done — fresh pasta takes only **2–3 minutes**.
2. Transfer the pasta into a skillet with a **portion of hot ragù**, a **splash of pasta water**, and **a knob of butter** (about 1 tsp per serving). Toss over low heat **a minute**, until every ribbon is coated. **Parmigiano at the table** — don't skip it.

## Tips

- **The solo rhythm:** sauce on day one, then each night is just boiling fresh pasta and tossing with a reheated ladleful — a 10-minute dinner off a 4-hour foundation. Reheat the sauce gently with a spoonful of water.
- **The whole recipe is patience management:** three evaporation stages (milk, wine, tomato reduction) that each must finish. Rushing any of them is the difference between good and transcendent.
- **No browning, no garlic, no herbs** — that's not an oversight, it's the recipe. The soffritto, nutmeg, and long simmer do all the work.
- **Fresh vs. dried:** fresh egg tagliatelle is the traditional match and clings best. Dried works too — ~90 g per serving, cooked very al dente, finished in the sauce.
- **Doubles cleanly** in the same pot, same timing, and freezes flat like the dal — the move for when you're home with a freezer.
- **The parmesan rind trick** works in any long simmer — save rinds in a bag in the freezer.
