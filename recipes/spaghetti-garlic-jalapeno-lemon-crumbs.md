# Spaghetti with Garlic, Jalapeño, Lemon & Sourdough Crumbs — Cook Mode

*Timing is the only hard part. The water and most of the knife work happen inside the crumb and garlic windows, and **the pasta goes in partway through the garlic fry** (Step 2) — the cue is written into the step. Once the wine is reduced, everything moves fast and nothing waits. **Not in the mood to juggle?** Step 5 lets you finish the whole sauce, park it off the heat, and boil the pasta afterwards — a few minutes longer, no timing at all.*

**Serves:** 4 | **Prep:** 5 min (the rest of the mise happens inside the cook) | **Cook:** ~35 min | **Total:** ~40 min

---

## 🛒 Grocery List

### Produce
- [ ] 1 head garlic (need 12 cloves — 1 smashed for the crumbs, 10 thinly sliced, 1 grated raw at the end)
- [ ] 1 jalapeño
- [ ] 1 large lemon (all the zest, 2 tbsp juice)
- [ ] 30 g flat-leaf parsley

### Bakery
- [ ] 3 thick slices sourdough, crust and all — *a sourdough olive loaf is the house upgrade*

### Meat & Dairy
- [ ] 70 g cold unsalted butter
- [ ] 90 g Parmigiano Reggiano (+ more for the table)
- [ ] 1–2 anchovy fillets, oil-packed — *house amount is 2* (a small tin or jar; the rest keeps)

### Pantry
- [ ] 400 g spaghetti
- [ ] Olive oil (9 tbsp total — 3 for the crumbs, 6 for the sauce, plus a drizzle to serve)
- [ ] 200 ml dry Pinot Grigio
- [ ] Fine salt (1½ tbsp for the pasta water) + kosher salt
- [ ] Black pepper
- [ ] Dried chilli flakes (½ tsp, optional)

### Optional Greens (genuinely skippable — the dish is complete without them)
- [ ] 10 g basil
- [ ] 10 g chives
- [ ] 50 g arugula

---

## Before You Start (5 Minutes — Only These Three Things)

Everything else gets prepped *inside* the cook, in the crumb and garlic windows. Don't front-load it; that's what turns a 40-minute dish into an hour.

- **Water on first:** fill a pot with about **3.5 L** water, add **1½ tbsp fine salt**, lid on, high heat. *(Less water than usual and slightly less salt than usual — starchier water builds the sauce, and the anchovy and parmesan bring their own salt.)*
- **Bread:** tear the **3 slices of sourdough** into rough chunks and pulse to coarse, irregular crumbs — rubble, not sand.
- **Garlic for the crumbs:** smash **1 clove**. Have a plate ready to tip the crumbs onto.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Crumbs (start this first, with the water heating)

1. Heat **3 tbsp olive oil** in a pan over **medium-low** with the **1 smashed garlic clove**. Add the crumbs and toss often, **6–8 minutes**, until deep gold and properly crisp — bite one; it should snap, not bend. Fresh sourdough holds moisture, so don't rush it on high heat.
2. Fish out the garlic, **salt immediately off the heat**, and tip onto a plate — not paper towel, they'll steam. Wipe the pan.
3. *While the crumbs toast (this is a real window — use it):* thinly slice **10 garlic cloves**, thinly slice the **jalapeño**, and roughly chop the **1–2 anchovy fillets**. These are the next three things you need.
4. When the water boils, drop it to a covered low boil so it doesn't boil away. Warm four serving bowls if you have room.

### Step 2 — Garlic and Jalapeño, Low and Slow (and the rest of your mise)

1. In a wide pan (30 cm+) over **LOW** heat, warm **6 tbsp olive oil** with the **sliced garlic** and **sliced jalapeño**. Cook gently **7–8 minutes**, until the slices are pale gold at the edges. Never brown — bitter garlic is unrecoverable and there's no saving the dish from it.
2. **⏱ Drop the spaghetti 2–3 minutes into this step** — when the slices have turned translucent and are sizzling steadily, before any colour. See the timing box below. *(Or skip the drop entirely and take the relaxed route in Step 5 — sauce first, pasta after.)*
3. *While the garlic cooks:* grate **90 g Parmigiano** on a microplane (fine grating is what lets it melt instead of clump), grate **1 large garlic clove**, zest and juice the **lemon**, chop **30 g parsley**, and cube **70 g cold butter** — straight back in the fridge until you need it. *If you're using the optional greens:* tear **10 g basil**, snip **10 g chives**, and roughly chop half the **arugula**.

> **⏱ When the pasta goes in**
> From the moment the sliced garlic hits the oil, the base is **~12 minutes** from finished: 7–8 min garlic + 1 min anchovy + 4 min wine. Your pasta needs **package time minus 2** — usually 9–10 minutes. Those two numbers meet **2–3 minutes into the garlic fry**.
> **If in doubt, go late rather than early.** The base holds happily (Step 5 parks it off the heat with a ladle of pasta water), and pasta can sit in its own hot water off the heat for a minute or two. Drained pasta waiting on a pan is the one thing that can't be fixed.

### Step 3 — Melt the Anchovy (1 minute)

1. Add the **chopped anchovy** and press it against the pan with your spoon. It dissolves completely in about a minute and won't read as fishy — it just deepens everything underneath. **Two fillets is the house amount**: more savoury depth, still nobody clocks it as fish.
2. Add **½ tsp chilli flakes** now if you want more kick, and swirl **30 seconds**.

### Step 4 — Reduce the Wine (4 minutes)

1. Turn the heat to **medium-high** and pour in **200 ml Pinot Grigio**. Reduce about **4 minutes**, until the pan is nearly dry and syrupy — you should be able to drag a spoon and see the pan floor for a second. Take it all the way; under-reduced wine tastes raw and sharp, and this is the step people cut short.

### Step 5 — Hold the Base

1. Kill the heat and add **a ladle of pasta water** to stop the base scorching. This is the buffer — it will sit here calmly for several minutes if your pasta needs longer.
2. *The relaxed route — no timing required:* don't drop the pasta during Step 2 at all. Take the sauce all the way to here, park it off the heat with its ladle of water, and **only then** boil the spaghetti. It costs about ten extra minutes of wall clock and removes every bit of arithmetic. The base is genuinely happy sitting; the only thing that can't wait is drained pasta.

### Step 6 — Emulsify (about 2 minutes)

1. When the pasta is **2 minutes short of package time**, reserve **2 cups of the water** and set it right next to the stove.
2. Tongs the pasta straight into the pan with **1 cup of pasta water** over medium-high. Toss and shake constantly **about 2 minutes**, until the liquid tightens into a glossy sauce that coats every strand. Splash in more water if it tightens too far. Keep the pasta spread across the pan, not piled — piled pasta won't emulsify.

### Step 7 — Butter and Parmesan, Off the Heat

1. Pull the pan **off the burner and count to twenty**.
2. Add the **cubed butter** and toss until melted. Then the **90 g parmesan** in three handfuls, tossing hard between each. Heat at this stage makes the parmesan clump and the sauce split.

### Step 8 — Wake It Up

1. Still off heat, stir through the **grated raw garlic clove**, **all the lemon zest and 2 tbsp of the juice**, the **parsley**, **½ tsp cracked black pepper**, and the **basil if you're using it**.
2. *If using arugula:* toss **half of it** through to just wilt — any longer and it turns bitter.
3. **Taste before salting.** Between the anchovy and the parmesan you may not need any.

### Step 9 — Serve Immediately

1. Twirl into the warm bowls. Top with the **toasted crumbs**, a thin drizzle of oil, and more parmesan — plus the **chives** and **remaining raw arugula** if you have them.
2. Crumbs go on at the table, never in the pan. Serve straight away — this waits for no one.

## Tips

- **The three that decide it:** garlic pale gold, never brown · wine reduced to almost nothing · parmesan added off the heat.
- **Why off the heat:** above roughly 70°C the parmesan's proteins seize and the fat splits out — a greasy pan with clumps in it instead of a sauce. Twenty seconds of patience is the whole trick.
- **Don't skip the crumbs.** They're not a garnish, they're the texture of the dish. A **sourdough olive loaf** makes them even better — the olive reads as another savoury layer alongside the anchovy.
- **The greens are optional, the parsley isn't.** Basil, chives, and arugula all skipped on the first cook and it was still a 5 — they're a lift, not a load-bearing part. Parsley, lemon, garlic, and crumbs are the ones doing the work.
- **If the sauce looks oily rather than creamy:** not enough agitation or not enough starch. Another splash of pasta water and toss harder — it will come together.
- **If it tastes flat:** more lemon juice before more salt. The anchovy and parmesan have usually covered the salt.
- **Leftovers:** not really a leftovers dish — the sauce breaks and the crumbs go soft. Cook what you'll eat. **Leftover crumbs** keep in a jar at room temperature for a few days and are good on anything.
